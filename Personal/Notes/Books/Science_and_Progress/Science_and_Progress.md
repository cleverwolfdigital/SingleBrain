## Premise

In a world where science and magic are the same profession, **Wizards** are inventors, philosophers,
and scientists who certify in colored disciplines. Twin 12-year-old brothers grow up in their
father's lab: the unnamed **narrator**, who loves fantasy and is slow to learn magic, and **Josiah**,
who is quiet, logical, and brilliant with technology.

Their father — holder of more certifications than anyone alive and creator of the Grey category —
hands each boy an object and tells them to keep it safe. Moments later the lab is attacked, the
father and Josiah are gone, and the **APO Incident** begins.

## The twist (already written, in Ch. 25)

The narrator caused the explosion. He becomes the **Dark Wizard**. His brother Josiah is the one who
has to stop him.

This means Chapter 1 is being told by an **unreliable narrator** — his hazy, blacked-out account of
the attack is concealing his own responsibility for it. Everything in between is his descent.

## The magic system (established in Ch. 1)

| Color | Domain |
|---|---|
| Green | Healing and Protection |
| Red | Fire and PK Strength (Telekinesis) |
| Blue | Water and Ice |
| Yellow | Wind and Earth |
| Black | Spatial Magic |
| White | Light |
| **Grey** | Light and Spatial Magic, to a lesser degree — **created by the father** |

**Rules already fixed:** wizards must train continuously to retain skill; the father holds 4 colored
certifications plus a Master's in Electrical Engineering from the University of Manoa; Keepers meet
twice monthly to review discoveries and rule on proposed new categories; the father is **Keeper of
the Grey**; the boys are publicly known as **"Children of the Gray."** The narrator cannot perform
grey magic — which the ending makes pointed.

**Also established:** Wizards, Mages, and Sorcerers are explicitly *different* things, and the
narrator notes this "came to be important later in my life." That is a planted setup with no payoff yet.
