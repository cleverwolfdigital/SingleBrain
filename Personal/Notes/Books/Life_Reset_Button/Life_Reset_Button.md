## What the copy commits the book to

Extracted from the above — these are the promises the manuscript has to keep:

- **Core thesis:** failure comes from *decision load*, not from lack of motivation or talent
- **Solution shape:** systems that are **simple**, **visible**, and **automated** (three named criteria)
- **Four pillars:** health, wealth, relationships, time
- **Repeatability:** you can reset *whenever you need to*, "without losing momentum" — the reset is
  a recurring tool, not a one-time event
- **Dual audience:** the multi-business operator *and* the merely-scattered reader

The project brief names **five**: health, wealth, relationships, **work**, **energy**.

Chapter 3 was written against the **five**, treating "time" as split across work and energy, and it
justifies energy-as-separate-from-health explicitly in the text. If the four-pillar version is
canonical, the back-cover copy and Ch. 3's domain sweep both need reconciling — and it's a
one-or-the-other call, not a merge.

## Open decisions before drafting

- **Is there a real outline?** Chapters 1–3 have titles and there are four named pillars, but no
  full table of contents exists. Chapter 3 ("Your Current State Audit") can't be written well
  without knowing what chapters 4+ do with the audit's output.
- **Voice:** the copy is direct second-person ("you'll learn", "stop reacting"). Confirm the
  chapters match that rather than drifting into memoir or case study.
- **Does the reader get artifacts?** "Simple, visible, automated" and an "audit" imply worksheets,
  templates, or a scorecard. Decide whether those ship inside chapters, in an appendix, or as a
  separate download.
- **How much draws on your own portfolio?** The "managing multiple businesses" audience is you.
  Whether the book uses real examples from the portfolio is a positioning choice worth making early.
